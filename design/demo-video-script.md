# MediBro — demo segment script (Congressional App Challenge)

**Length:** about 1:25, roughly 210 words at a relaxed 150 words a minute.
**Speakers:** **A** = you, **B** = your co-founder (fewer lines). Each scene is one voice, so the hand-offs fall on scene cuts.
**Look:** a phone screen in the middle, with rounded corners and a soft drop shadow. Behind it, a slow, dark, fluid gradient. Short captions sit beside the phone.

---

## 0. The problem (0:00–0:13)
**Screen:** dark gradient only. A slow zoom into the tiny print of a sample label (shorthand and small warning stickers). Two stat cards fade in beside it, one per sentence.
**Captions:** "46% misread a common label instruction" · "25 million+ Americans have limited English"
**Sources (check before publishing):** Davis et al., "Literacy and Misunderstanding Prescription Drug Labels," *Annals of Internal Medicine* (2006); U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey (people 5+ who speak English less than "very well").
> **A:** Prescription labels are full of tiny print and pharmacy shorthand. Nearly half of patients misread at least one common label instruction, and over 25 million Americans have limited English. That's a real risk.

## 1. Intro (0:13–0:17)
**Screen:** the label shrinks into the phone as it rises into the center. The welcome screen shows the logo, then "MediBro".
> **A:** Here's how MediBro fixes that.

## 2. Setup (0:17–0:24)
**Screen:** one question per screen: name → pharmacy → text size Large → picking a color, which recolors the app live.
> **B:** Setup takes seconds: your name, your pharmacy, bigger text if you want it, and your favorite color.

## 3. Read a label (0:24–0:42)
**Screen:** three bottle photos → reading → plain directions → the language switches to Español → Listen highlights each line.
**Captions:** "Reads on the phone" · "6 languages" · "Never guesses"
> **A:** To read a label, take a few photos as you turn the bottle. MediBro reads it right on the phone, turns the shorthand into plain words in six languages, and reads it aloud. If it isn't sure, it shows the label as printed instead of guessing.

## 4. Refills (0:42–0:49)
**Screen:** "3 days left" → the "Time to refill" note → tap Call pharmacy.
> **B:** It tracks how many days you have left, reminds you to refill, and calls your pharmacy in one tap.

## 5. Check a pill (0:49–0:59)
**Screen:** "Should look like" → a pill photo → color ✓, shape ✓, stamp confirmed → "This pill matches."
> **B:** Before taking a pill, photograph it. MediBro checks the color, shape and stamp against the FDA's description, and warns you if it doesn't match.

## 6. Voice assistant (0:59–1:15)
**Screen:** one tap on the orb → "I have a headache" → the spoken answer card → quick cuts to the blood-thinner warning, chest pain → 911, and the "Old medicines" steps.
**Captions:** "Over-the-counter only" · "Not medical advice"
> **A:** Tap the orb and ask about a symptom. It answers out loud with over-the-counter options only, checks your allergies and medicines, sends emergencies to 911, and explains how to dispose of old medicines.

## 7. Built (1:15–1:22)
**Screen:** the phone eases left as three cards stack on the right: "React · TypeScript · Swift", "Data stays on the phone", "372 automated tests".
> **B:** Your data stays on your phone. It's built with React, TypeScript and Swift, and backed by 372 automated tests.

## 8. Close (1:22–1:25)
**Screen:** the phone back on Home. The logo and name fade in.
> **A:** I'm [your name]…
> **B:** …and I'm [co-founder's name]…
> **A + B:** …and this is MediBro.
